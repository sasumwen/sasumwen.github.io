<p class="lead">In July 2025 the Digital Research Alliance of Canada sent ten of us to the International HPC Summer School in Lisbon. For a week we learned how the world's largest machines are programmed, and I presented the segmentation work from my thesis to people who think in petaflops.</p>

## Why a medical-imaging student went to a supercomputing school

A brain scan is a volume, not a picture, and a network that reads volumes is hungry for memory and time. The summer school, held from 6 to 11 July, is where graduate students from several countries go to learn the other side of that problem: parallel programming, performance, and what changes when the machine is no longer the limit.

## Presenting BoRefAttnNet

I took BoRefAttnNet with me, the boundary-refined 3D network at the centre of [my thesis](/research/thesis/), and presented it during the week. It was a month before my defence, and the audience came from every field except mine, so it was useful practice at explaining why the edges of a small brain structure are worth the extra effort.

The school pairs every student with a mentor. Mine was Rabiya Noori, and I am grateful for her time and guidance.
