<p class="lead">It was Black History Month, and I was honoured to be recognised as one of this year's seven Black founders carving a niche in Canada's innovation ecosystem, by the Black Business Ventures Association, at the 2026 Innovation Expo at Platform Calgary.</p>

The evening had a run-up. In an invite-only founders' session, Brandy Old and Craig Elias walked us through founder traction and ecosystem positioning: where a young company sits in the landscape and how to say so without flinching. Then an insightful panel with Damilare O. Odumosu, Henry Madubuobi and Daniella Jasper-Okumagba, whose perspectives on leadership and resilience resonated deeply.

## The showcase

At the main-stage showcase, Xyricon presented Auron: a real-time, edge-AI emergency-response co-pilot designed to eliminate "dead air" in public-safety answering points, the silence a caller hears when every dispatcher is already on a line.

Presenting it to a room of founders, investors and ecosystem builders, rather than to engineers, is its own kind of test: does the idea land without the jargon? Auron is easy to explain for a reason. Someone is calling for help, nobody is free to answer, and the product exists so that the line is never silent: triage at the edge, with a human always in charge.

<PullQuote>Innovation is as much about people as it is about technology.</PullQuote>

## Thank you

I connected with some of the most inspiring founders, investors and ecosystem builders from across Canada, and every conversation was a reminder of that line above. A special appreciation to Moustafa Osman for his constant nudging and belief in what we are building, and to Ayo Aiyeleye and Dipo Alli, at the helm of BBVA, for their unwavering support and leadership. Grateful as well to Reza Ardestani of RadarCX AI for showing up in the middle of his own build, and to everyone who continues to believe in Xyricon's vision of trusted, human-in-the-loop AI for public safety.
