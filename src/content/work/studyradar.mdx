## Context

StudyRadar helps pharmacy and healthcare candidates prepare for high-stakes licensing examinations: OSCE stations, evaluating exams and multiple-choice papers. It is an Alberta company, and it describes itself as Canada's first integrated AI platform for pharmacists, physicians and nurses.

## My role

I joined StudyRadar in December 2024 as a machine-learning engineer, on contract, while finishing my MSc. I became Technical Project Lead in September 2025 as the team grew, and was appointed Chief Technology Officer in July 2026. Today I lead the company's technical strategy and AI development.

## What we built

- A web and mobile learning platform with peer-reviewed question banks and tailored practice.
- A **voice-based OSCE simulator**: a candidate speaks with an AI patient, and an AI examiner then assesses the encounter and gives feedback the candidate can act on.
- Tools that let teachers, tutors and institutions run StudyRadar for their own students.

## How

The questions and clinical scenarios are written and reviewed by licensed healthcare professionals; the AI does the tireless part, playing the standardised patient and the assessor at any hour. Architecture detail is kept at the level StudyRadar itself publishes.

## Working with Amii

In July 2026 StudyRadar was named to the ninth cohort of [Level Up](https://www.amii.ca/courses/level-up), the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute's year-long programme for AI startups. As CTO I lead our technical sessions with Amii's machine-learning scientists: we bring what we have built and what is not yet working, and they ask the questions we had not thought to ask ourselves. The habit I have kept from those sessions is a simple one. Before you trust a measurement, test the tool doing the measuring.

## What I learned

The first time an AI system holds a spoken conversation, it feels like magic. Once students rely on it before an exam, the questions change. Did it hear the student correctly? Is the feedback useful and consistent? Is it quick enough to feel natural? Are sensitive details handled responsibly? A beautiful interface cannot answer those questions. Testing, careful design and sound engineering can.

So the lesson is reliability over novelty: evaluation, fallbacks and honest feedback matter more than any single model. And growing a team taught me to write things down early: architecture, conventions, and the reasons behind them.
