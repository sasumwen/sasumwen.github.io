## Context

In 2021 Nigeria's startup community and the Presidency set out to write a law together. Nigerian engineers were already competing on the global stage, and the country was Africa's largest economy, but regulatory uncertainty kept scaring investors away. The Bill was meant to give technology companies a clear legal footing: definitions, incentives, a seed fund, regulatory sandboxes and a council to keep the dialogue between government and founders alive. I joined the drafting team as a specialist consultant on drafting and review.

## What I worked on

- Drafting and review across the Bill's provisions, with a particular interest in how it treats venture capital and investment vehicles. That was the subject of a workshop presentation I gave to the Federal Ministry of Communications and Digital Economy in 2021, later published as [an article](/writing/nigeria-startup-bill-and-venture-capital/).
- Stakeholder consultation rounds with founders, investors and public officials.

## What it offers an investor

The part of the Bill I wrote about publicly is the part that de-risks investment:

- **A tax credit** on company income worth 30% of what an investor puts into a registered startup.
- **No capital gains tax** on the profit when angel investors, venture capitalists or private equity funds sell their stake in a registered startup.
- **Guaranteed repatriation** of dividends, profits and sale proceeds for foreign investors, in freely convertible currency.
- **A National Council for Digital Innovation and Entrepreneurship** to carry the policy and keep it consistent.

## Outcome

The Nigeria Startup Act was signed into law on 19 October 2022. In December 2022 I received a Presidential Certificate of Recognition for contributions to the Act, issued through the Act's secretariat and signed by the Senior Special Assistant to the President on Digital Transformation, who led the Act.

## What it taught me

Policy is engineering with a longer feedback loop. The habits transfer: define the terms, think about edge cases, and write for the people who will have to live inside the system.
