abstract: >-
  Accurate segmentation and detection in neuroimaging is essential for advancing clinical
  understanding and the diagnosis of schizophrenia. This thesis introduces Boundary-Refined
  Attention Network (BoRefAttnNet), a novel boundary-refined 3D U-Net variant specifically designed
  for precise segmentation of subcortical brain structures from structural magnetic resonance
  imaging (sMRI). BoRefAttnNet incorporates multi-scale boundary attention modules that explicitly
  highlight anatomically critical edges while suppressing background noise, significantly improving
  segmentation accuracy for small or complex anatomical structures. Evaluations using
  FastSurfer-processed sMRI data from the publicly available Centre for Biomedical Research
  Excellence (COBRE) dataset demonstrate that BoRefAttnNet significantly outperforms conventional
  3D U-Net baselines in accurately delineating key subcortical structures, including the
  hippocampus, amygdala, and basal ganglia. Building upon this enhanced segmentation capability, we
  further experiment with a Dynamic Spatial-Temporal Transformer Model (DySTTM) to detect
  schizophrenia by integrating structural and functional MRI (fMRI) modalities. The DySTTM
  leverages spatial attention to capture anatomical interdependencies from segmented sMRI data and
  temporal attention to model dynamic brain connectivity patterns from resting-state fMRI.
  Experimental results indicate that the integration of these multimodal imaging features using
  DySTTM provides superior diagnostic accuracy and interpretability compared to established models
  such as 3D ResNet and XGBoost classifiers.

## The question

Schizophrenia leaves subtle traces in the brain: small differences in the size and shape of subcortical structures, and differences in how regions talk to each other over time. Measuring either reliably depends on segmentation that is accurate at the edges of small structures, and on models that can read structure and function together.

## Two contributions

**BoRefAttnNet** addresses the first problem. It is a 3D U-Net whose decoder carries multi-scale *boundary attention modules*: at each resolution the network is pushed to represent where a structure ends, not only where it is. On FastSurfer-processed sMRI from the public COBRE dataset it outperformed conventional 3D U-Net baselines, most clearly at the boundary.

**DySTTM**, the Dynamic Spatial-Temporal Transformer Model, addresses the second. It takes the segmented structural representation and resting-state fMRI, applies spatial attention to anatomical interdependencies and temporal attention to the evolving connectivity patterns, fuses the two, and classifies. Against 3D ResNet and XGBoost classifiers the multimodal model gave the best accuracy and ROC-AUC.

## Results

The data is the public COBRE release: 174 people, 88 diagnosed with schizophrenia and 86 controls, split 70 / 15 / 15 into training, validation and test sets.

## How it was built

Models were trained on a Google Cloud instance with four NVIDIA L4 GPUs. Segmentation was evaluated with Dice, Hausdorff distance and average surface distance; detection with accuracy, precision, recall, F1 and ROC-AUC. Data came from the Centre for Biomedical Research Excellence (COBRE) release, pre-processed with FastSurfer.
